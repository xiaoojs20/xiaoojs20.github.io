Thoughts While Basking in the Sun by Erhai Lake: A Personal Meiji Restoration and a Family Reform and Opening-up

Published 2026-10-29 22:16 Written 2026.10 4 minutes read
简体中文

Author: Liquid Stack

Source: https://xiaoojs20.github.io/p/erhai-reflections/

At the beginning of 2026, I hoped to accomplish a personal Meiji Restoration within three years and a family reform and opening-up within five.

At the heart of the Meiji Restoration is a revolution in capability. In a traditional society, the signals that guide a person’s life tend to come from the outside. Today, we need to establish a central government of our own: improve our understanding, learn to make decisions, and manage ourselves. We need personal infrastructure, whether that means technical skills for work, investment knowledge and accounts, legal identity and household registration, connections, or a lean, muscular physique. Before railways are built, even an ambitious country cannot develop real productive capacity. Likewise, without such infrastructure, even the grandest personal values are difficult to put into practice. We need the spirit of the Iwakura Mission: acknowledge how much we do not know about the world beyond us, and actively go out and see it. Build our knowledge inwardly, and explore this blue planet outwardly.

At the heart of reform and opening-up is an institutional revolution. One direction for reform is to reduce the excessive concentration and personalization of power, and strengthen collective leadership, shared responsibilities, and institutional governance. It means changing how a family operates: moving from authority and convention toward transparency, incentives, consultation, experimentation, and openness. A modern family should allow these matters to be brought back into discussion: how family assets are allocated, how responsibilities are shared among members, decisions about having children, and care for aging parents. Rather than asking everyone to follow the same path in life, a family can acknowledge its members’ differences and seek a sustainable way to cooperate over the long term.

Our generation’s personal modernization is taking place just as broader economic tailwinds are weakening and the external order is growing more uncertain. For ordinary people, many assumptions that could once be taken for granted now need to be tested again. Can a degree reliably lead to a career? Can a job last ten years? Does buying a home necessarily build wealth? Will an industry’s core skills today still be scarce five years from now? Is a city worth twenty years of one’s life?

The future is a time when old answers are gradually losing their force and new ones have yet to emerge. We need to keep rebuilding the infrastructure for our own survival and development. We must build our own systems of knowledge, because formal education cannot cover everything we will need to learn throughout life. We must cultivate our own career capital, because no organization can promise permanent security…

“In this era, financial independence is easier to achieve than ever before. Your income will soon exceed your parents’, and in another five to ten years, your assets may exceed what they accumulated over a lifetime. This is a completely asymmetric battle; there is no need to fight it. They will compromise.” When someone still relies on family for housing, income, a sense of security, and social resources, their freedom is naturally incomplete. People often say that good memories compound, too. A person’s Meiji Restoration is never truly finished. We can be imperfect, and we can remain uncertain and rebuild, but at the very least we should learn to take responsibility for our unfinished selves, rather than making a partner bear the cost of our own disorder.

A good partnership is, at its core, a source of mutual growth rather than mutual rescue. Alone, a person has one set of experiences, one perspective, and one life trajectory. Together, two people can share memories, exchange judgments, and correct each other’s blind spots, giving both more security and possibility than either might have alone. More importantly, over a long enough time, a partner becomes a witness to your life: someone who knows what you believed when you were young, understands why you changed, and has seen your ambition, exhaustion, failures, and fresh starts.

To the bright future.
