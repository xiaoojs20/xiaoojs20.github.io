For young people, **the AI era is almost the era of lean muscle**. AI is changing three things at once: **the relative price of standardized cognitive output, the way people are tied to work, and the cost of looking after one's body.** Taken together, these changes make it worthwhile to move physical training up the list of things that used to come after work.

## 1. Ordinary, easily copied cognitive work will lose value quickly

When the reaper arrived, people who swung scythes did not become weaker. But harvesting the same amount of grain no longer required as many workers. **Every technological shift makes some paid tasks cheaper and turns other abilities or resources into new bottlenecks.** Here is one way to look at the transition from agriculture to the AI era.

| Transition | What gets cheaper | What becomes scarce | What not to bet on | What to build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agriculture → industry | Repetitive physical work that can be mechanized | Machines, capital, and the ability to organize production at scale | More effort and longer hours | Operate and maintain equipment; organize people to produce with it |
| Industry → information age | Routine tasks that can be written as rules | Turning business processes into software and using data to solve complex problems | Memorizing procedures, manual spreadsheets, repetitive data entry | Turn recurring processes into tools and handle exceptions |
| Information age → AI era | First drafts of cognitive work that can be described in natural language and checked quickly | Defining problems, verifying what ships, taking responsibility for results, proprietary data, and customer access | Hand-writing every line of code, piling up deliverables, proving commitment by sitting at a desk | Build judgment through practice, hand repetitive work to agents, and save time for your body |

- Machines took over hand harvesting and carrying. Software took over manual bookkeeping, order entry, and reports assembled from fixed templates. The share of the U.S. workforce employed in agriculture fell from about 41% in 1900 to 1.9% in 2000. Mechanization alone did not cause this, and these figures do not measure farm workers' wage losses, but they show the scale of labor moving out of agriculture. [Historical statistics from the U.S. Department of Agriculture](https://www.ers.usda.gov/media/8254/eib-3.pdf?v=71607)

- Computers reduced the labor needed for routine manual and cognitive tasks while increasing the relative demand for nonroutine cognitive work. [Research by Autor, Levy, and Murnane](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552801)

- The most important difference with AI is this: **software used to need rules written into it before it could act. Now, if a goal can be explained and the result checked, some work can go to an agent even when it is hard to describe with a complete set of rules.**

**Coding agents are starting to change how internet companies think about staffing.** Fixing bugs, adding tests, and building small features used to mean adding people. Now an engineer who knows the business can hand several tasks to different agents to work on in parallel, then review the results. Over the next few years, junior roles focused mainly on execution may feel the pressure first. In 2026, AI has begun taking part in research on frontier mathematics, with research teams reporting solutions to open problems. [OpenAI's report on mathematical advances](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/)

- **Some work that once needed a large team may shift to a few core engineers and several agents.** Core engineers decide what to build, judge whether the results are ready to ship, and take responsibility for them. For young people, opportunities to develop that judgment may become the scarce resource.

- **The code and documents belong to the company. Your lean muscle belongs to you.** Work products need to be handed over. Your physique does not.

## 2. Across five thousand years, power and sexual appeal remain reliable answers

Technology changes, as do the things we can mass-produce, but two abilities are harder to copy. **One is deciding what other people get. The other is making people willing to pay a price to get close to you.** The first is power. The second is attraction, with sexual appeal as one direct form of it.

**Power**

- **During the Warring States period, Lü Buwei bet on the offices Zichu might one day award.** When Zichu was still a hostage in Zhao, Lü judged him a “rare commodity,” spent money cultivating contacts, and arranged for people to persuade Lady Huayang to make him heir. Once Zichu took the throne, Lü became chancellor.
- **Today, someone seeking promotion might first give a gift to Yu Lifang, the wife of a senior official.** She held no formal human-resources post, yet could directly approach provincial and municipal leaders to seek promotions for others. Others competed for a place on the list; she could influence the list. [A report by China's Central Commission for Discipline Inspection](https://m.ccdi.gov.cn/content/5b/1a/12667.html)

**Sexual appeal | From being noticed to getting what others cannot**

- **In ancient China, Emperor Xuanzong favored Yang Guifei.** Her three sisters were made ladies of the state, and her cousin Yang Guozhong rose to prominence. What mattered was not that everyone found her beautiful, but that the person who did could bring status to her family.
- **Today, people may spend money without ever meeting in person.** In a 2026 interview, OnlyFans creator Sophie Rain confirmed that one fan she knew only through the platform had spent nearly $5 million on her over 11 months. [Interview transcript](https://podcastrex.com/shows/the-bryce-crawford-podcast/onlyfans-star-sophie-rain-says-jesus-is-her-best-friend/transcript). Some pay to view content; others pay for a few more replies from her.

**Power decides who gets to make the call. Sexual appeal can make people come closer, even spend money.** But there is a more direct reason the body is valuable: even if nobody is watching, the strength you build is still yours to use.

## 3. You can check in with an agent between sets, so training need not interrupt work

Work and training used to collide because many tasks could only move forward at a computer. When an experiment finished, I had to inspect the results, change parameters, and submit the next run myself. Now I can finish a set of rows, pick up my phone during the rest, check what an agent just completed, and reply, “Keep the original data split and add a comparison.” I put the phone away and do the next set; the agent keeps editing code and running experiments remotely. **I need a few seconds to make a decision, not to sit back down at a computer and do the whole next round from scratch.**

This is no longer just hypothetical. With Codex, for example, you can send instructions from your phone, adjust a running task, and review changes and test results. [Codex on mobile](https://openai.com/index/work-with-codex-from-anywhere/). Sending an instruction, answering a clear question, or checking an interim result can fit into a rest period. Close code review, diagnosing an anomaly, and taking responsibility for final acceptance can wait until I am back at my computer. Not every task belongs on a phone, but many no longer need to wait for me to return to my desk before they can move forward.

That gives me one less excuse to put off going to the gym. I used to think I had to finish this round before leaving. Now I can go train, then check in when an agent finishes if I have time between sets. **I keep training, and work keeps moving. What shrinks is the time I have to spend at my computer.**

## 4. AI narrows information gaps, helping ordinary people train and eat well

**You may not need a coach or nutritionist to train and eat better now.** If I can get to the gym only two evenings a week, I can send my training log to AI and ask what to do next time. At lunch, I can send it the cafeteria menu and ask how to put this meal together, instead of receiving a meal plan I cannot use.

The internet has never lacked workout tutorials. What is missing is someone who can tell me what to do today with my schedule, the equipment I have, and the dishes in front of me. **AI narrows that information gap. If an ordinary person wants to start, they can do a set and put together a good meal today. The hardest part is still doing it again tomorrow.**

## 5. Why muscle, and why lean?

**Muscle is function.** The benefits of resistance training are practical: building muscle can support metabolism, protect body tissues, and make the body more resilient. [The World Health Organization on physical activity](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity/)

**For young people just starting their careers, these years may be their last golden window for building muscle.** After graduation, commuting, overtime, meals, and sleep are all being rearranged. If training is always scheduled for “after work is done today,” a few years can turn into a habit of waiting until life gets less busy. If instead you treat two or three weekly sessions as part of a normal schedule, what you build over these years is not just muscle and strength, but also a routine you know how to keep when work gets busy.

**Lean means low maintenance, leaving the main plot of life intact.** Defined shoulders, back, and waist, plus a strong body, can be maintained with a few workouts a week and ordinary meals. When work gets busy, you can train less for a while; when traveling, you do not have to plan every day around a gym.

Managing blood sugar and body fat has direct benefits, too: it may help reduce inflammation and metabolic risk, lower the risk of cavities, and make muscle definition more visible.
